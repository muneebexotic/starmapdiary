A quiet observatory

Every frame below is the same running interface, not a picture of one. Write in it, tap a star, open the log, hide the interface. Warm ink, a single accent borrowed from the sky's own gold, and one surface visible at a time.

Phone · at rest

Two things at rest: a line to write on, and one dot to turn everything off. The date and the run sit above the field and are also the way into the log.

Phone · the log

The 28-night history and the date filter were the same object all along. Tapping a night filters the sky to it; the streak is a sentence underneath, not a scoreboard.

Phone · reading a night

No card, no title bar, no close X in the corner. The sky stills, the star you touched stays lit, and the writing rises next to it. Earlier and later nights are one tap away.

Phone · signed out

No locked composer to shake at you. The sky is free to look at; the account is a name, a sentence and two ruled lines within thumb reach.

Desktop

Same interface, more room. The log becomes a standing panel on the left rather than a sheet. Hovering a star shows its first eighty characters; opening one draws a hairline from the star out to the writing, the way an annotated chart names what you are looking at.
